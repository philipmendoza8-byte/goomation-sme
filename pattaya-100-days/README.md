## What this is

This is a field record of a 100-day assignment in Pattaya / Chonburi during 2026.

The record did not begin as a theory.

It began with work: vehicles, materials, people, photographs, route information, booking details, product information, work-in-progress, operating proof, and the repeated need to check whether what appeared in a file or on a platform matched what existed in the field.

The evidence now available separates the 100 days into two connected periods:

- **First 60 days — Pattaya field / KOOL Base**
- **Following 40 days — OTA work and Golden Dragon operations**

The purpose of this repository section is not to make those periods look cleaner than they were. It is to preserve what can actually be supported by records.

## What changed across the 100 days

The records support a shift in working attention.

At the beginning, much of the evidence is close to physical production:

**vehicle → material → measurement → wiring → assembly → correction**

Later, more of the evidence is close to service operations and digital products:

**vehicle → route → booking → pickup → service proof → OTA product → image / information check**

The common element is not a software platform.

The common element is the need to keep the digital description tied to the real thing.

A photograph can look good and still be the wrong photograph.

A vehicle can exist and still be matched to the wrong product option.

A route can be written down and still fail a field check.

A platform listing can be complete and still not describe the operation accurately.

The field keeps the record honest.

## Working method that survived the assignment

Later CV records summarize the working method as:

1. **Start with the field**
2. **Separate evidence from gaps**
3. **Organize for use**
4. **Use AI by task**
5. **Human check → working output**

And the operating principle is recorded as:

`FIELD → EVIDENCE → HUMAN CHECK → CONTENT OR WORKING SYSTEM → WORKING OUTCOME`

This repository does not claim that this entire formulation existed fully formed on day one.

It records that the formulation is consistent with the work and evidence that survived from the assignment.

## Why keep this

Because field memory disappears quickly.

A folder full of photographs can become noise.

A chat can lose its context.

A platform can change.

A team can remember the same event differently.

The surviving record is useful when it can still answer a simple question:

**What actually happened, and what evidence supports that statement?**

That is enough for this draft.
